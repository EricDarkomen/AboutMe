Long Fall

A sci-fi story game and colony builder · HTML, CSS and JavaScript · no build step

Lured to the spaceport city of a dying world, you wake four hundred years later on a drowned island off Cornwall in 1631, with seven months missing and a radio speaking in your mother’s voice. Then you build a world back up, from flint to rockets.

Underneath the story it is an operations game. The colony runs in real time on the game’s clock: every colonist has a job, every building has a production queue, materials are carried from the store to the site a load at a time, and the food goes off at dusk. Ten ages of industry, each one built from what the age before it could make.

It is the project I am working on now, and the one I point at when somebody asks what I can actually build. It runs in any browser, opens straight off a memory stick with no server, and ships with its own level editor.

Three decisions worth explaining

The tech tree is checked, not trusted

Every step of every age has to be makeable from what that age can have. A checker walks the whole tree on each change and catches loops a person would miss — tin that needs a trading stone that costs bronze.

No build step, on purpose

Modern JavaScript modules are fetched, and a page opened off a local disk is not allowed to fetch. Plain scripts are loaded. So the game opens from a folder with no server, no install and no toolchain.

Licence and credit as part of the build

The artwork is third-party kits, cut and packed by scripts that keep only what the game names. Every kit is credited with its provenance, and the story bible stays out of the published build by rule, not by memory.
